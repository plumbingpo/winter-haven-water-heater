Clear Pricing

Honest, Upfront Pricing

Before we pick up a wrench, we find out exactly what's wrong. Our technicians look at your heater's age and gallon capacity, the burner or elements, the relief valve, the venting, and any rust or seepage around the fittings. After that you get an honest recommendation and the full price up front, whether the job is replacing a thermocouple or replacing the whole unit. No hidden fees, no last-minute add-ons.

A lot of water heater failures give quiet warnings long before the tank quits. Keep an eye out for discolored or rusty hot water, popping noises when the burner fires, a sulfur smell from the hot tap, or a hot water supply that runs out faster than before. Behind those symptoms are usually a thick layer of sediment, a depleted anode rod, or a failing dip tube. A quick inspection at the first sign often costs far less than mopping up after a ruptured tank.

How old is your water heater? The serial number on the label usually tells us. When a tank gets past the 10-year mark, it's worth planning a replacement before the current one decides for you. As a rough guide, a 40 to 50 gallon gas tank typically runs $1,400–$2,800 installed, and an electric tank typically runs $1,100–$2,400, depending on venting, code updates, and access. We will tell you plainly whether yours has years left or is running on borrowed time.

Preventive Care

Water Heater Maintenance That Adds Years

Routine maintenance is the most reliable way to keep hot water flowing all year. Regular service heads off failures before they start, clears out sediment, and catches small faults while they're still cheap to fix. At Winter Haven Water Heater, every maintenance visit is built around your actual heater, covering a full tank flush, an anode rod inspection, a test of the temperature and pressure relief valve, a look at the burner and flue on gas models, element and thermostat testing on electric units, and a thermostat setting check.

No two maintenance plans look exactly alike. An older tank needs a closer look than a new one, a tankless unit on hard water calls for annual descaling to keep its heat exchanger clear, and a large family cycles the tank far harder than a couple does. We build each visit around your equipment and water, so every tune-up keeps the unit efficient and protects the equipment you've already paid for.

Think of maintenance as protection for equipment you've already bought. Each visit helps keep the tank clear of sediment, the anode rod doing its job, and the relief valve in working order, which lowers what you pay to heat water while reducing wear on the burner or elements. For most households, the plan costs far less than an early replacement.

Proper Sizing

A Water Heater Sized for Your Actual Household

Bigger isn't always better when it comes to water heaters. What matters for a tank is the first-hour rating, how many gallons of hot water it can deliver in one busy hour, and for a tankless unit it's the GPM it can hold at your incoming water temperature. That's why we never just copy the gallon rating of the old heater. We look at how many people live in the home, how many bathrooms you have, and big draws like a soaking tub or a high-capacity washer.

Choosing a new water heater is about more than the number of gallons. Our technician will compare tankless units against a traditional tank, and gas against electric or hybrid, walking through operating costs, venting and gas line requirements, electrical capacity, and the installation itself. Tankless works well for many households, though it often requires a bigger gas line and new venting, and a properly sized tank is often the simpler, less expensive option. The decision is yours, based on your home and budget.

Common Problems

Hot Water Problems We Solve Every Week

A cold tap where hot water should be, a pilot that won't stay lit, a rumbling, sediment-heavy tank, a weeping relief valve: every one of these problems has a different cause and calls for a different repair. Winter Haven Water Heater diagnoses the exact fault before replacing anything, whether it's an electric tank that trips its breaker, a gas heater with a failing thermocouple, a broken dip tube sending cold water straight to the tap, or a tankless unit throwing error codes.

Not every water heater problem is equally urgent. A bad element just cuts your hot water supply. A relief valve that discharges, on the other hand, signals that pressure or temperature inside the tank is too high, and a slow leak at the base can quietly ruin flooring before anyone notices. Smell gas? Leave the house and call your gas utility before anything else. We come prepared to swap elements, thermostats, thermocouples, gas valves, and relief valves, add a thermal expansion tank when pressure is the culprit, and handle leak repair at the drain valve or fittings.
