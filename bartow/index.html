Keeping Your Bartow Water Heater Running Longer

A little routine care makes all the difference between a water heater that runs for years and one that fails early. Our advice for homeowners is a short list: an annual sediment flush, an anode rod check every three to five years, a quick relief valve test once a year, a temperature setting around 120 degrees, and annual descaling for tankless units on hard water, so problems are found while they're still inexpensive.

Take a look at the age of your water heater. The rating plate on the tank usually includes the manufacture date, and that date says a lot about what comes next. Most tank heaters make it to 8 to 12 years, heat pump models 10 to 15, and regularly descaled tankless units about 20. If your heater is past that range and is also showing rusty water, rumbling, or dampness under the tank, it's usually due for a planned replacement, which is almost always cheaper and less stressful than an emergency replacement after a leak.

If warning signs do show up, acting quickly keeps them small. A dripping relief valve might point to high water pressure or thermal expansion with nowhere to go, both modest fixes. The same drip left alone for a season can wear out the valve and stress the tank until a seam gives way, and a small repair becomes a full replacement plus water damage. It's smart to keep our number saved on your phone so you can reach us at the first sign of trouble. Early intervention saves time, money, and cold showers.
