Most tankless problems announce themselves the same way, with a blinking code on the display and a shower that turns cold. Behind that code is a system with very little margin for error. There is no storage tank to fall back on, so the heater has to detect flow, light a burner or power its elements, and raise the water temperature by fifty degrees or more in the few seconds it takes water to cross the heat exchanger. When scale narrows the passages, when the flow sensor stops reading, when the flame rod cannot confirm a flame, or when the vent or gas line cannot keep up, the unit protects itself by shutting down. That is the pattern we see week after week in Winter Haven, FL.

Winter Haven Water Heater starts every tankless repair by reading the unit, not guessing at it. On every service call in Winter Haven, we pull the active error code and the stored fault history, then test the components that code points to: flow sensor output while a faucet runs, flame rod signal in microamps, igniter resistance, inlet gas pressure both static and at full fire, and vent condition from the unit to the termination. Scale gets checked by comparing inlet and outlet temperatures against flow rate, since a heat exchanger that cannot transfer heat gives itself away in those numbers. Error codes narrow the search, but they rarely name the broken part outright. An ignition code, for example, can come from a worn igniter, a dirty flame rod, low gas pressure, or a blocked intake, and each one calls for a different fix.

The good news is that most tankless problems are very fixable. A descaling flush clears most mineral buildup, sensors and igniters are replaceable parts, and venting faults usually come down to a blocked intake or a loose joint. Warning Signs to Watch For

Most tanks announce trouble weeks or months ahead with rumbling noises, rusty water, or hot showers that keep getting shorter. If any of the following sound familiar, it's worth having the unit checked:

  • Cold slugs in the middle of hot water that last longer than before
  • A clicking igniter with no flame, or a flame that lights and quickly drops out
  • A combustion fan that sounds louder or rougher than normal
  • Moisture, rust, or drips appearing below the heater
  • Hot water that fluctuates even though nobody touched the faucet
  • Small draws, like a bathroom sink, that never trigger the heater
  • Hot water that cannot keep up with more than one fixture
  • An error code flashing on the unit's display

Why Professional Tankless Water Heater Repair Matters

From the outside a tankless heater looks like a plain box; inside, it is a tightly controlled system. One fault code often has several possible causes, and the sensors, igniters, and control boards involved are not cheap to buy on a hunch. Gas models add real hazards: the burner can fire at close to 200,000 BTU per hour, the vent carries combustion gases that contain carbon monoxide, and a loose gas connection or a vent joint that separates can put fuel or exhaust into the living space. Electric tankless units carry their own risk, often drawing well over 100 amps across several 240-volt circuits, so a loose lug or undersized wire can overheat quickly.

A trained technician with proper test equipment gets to the answer faster. Ours read the fault history, measure flame signal and igniter resistance, test flow sensor output, and check gas pressure under full load with a manometer, which is the only reliable way to catch a gas line that holds pressure at idle but sags when the burner ramps up. Descaling is done with a circulating pump and the proper solution, followed by a thorough rinse. Just as important, you get an honest opinion: if the heat exchanger is damaged on an older unit, you will hear it from us before you spend money on a repair.

Water Heater Care Tips From Our Technicians

A few preventive steps can help you avoid a cold shower or a wet floor down the road. Follow these tips from our technicians:

  • If scale comes back quickly, add a softener or a scale-prevention cartridge on the cold inlet
  • Write down any error code before resetting the unit, and call if it keeps coming back
  • Have the gas line evaluated whenever you add a range, dryer, or fireplace
  • Never cut power to an outdoor or garage unit in winter, because it needs electricity to protect itself from freezing
  • Clean the inlet water filter screen a few times a year so the flow sensor gets full flow
  • Clear debris, snow, and dryer lint away from the vent and intake openings
  • Schedule a descaling flush every year; hard water may call for one every six months
